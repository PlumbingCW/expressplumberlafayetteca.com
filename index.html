Pipe burst or sewer backing up? Do this first

Water moves fast and does the most damage in the first few minutes. These steps cut the damage while you call a licensed emergency plumber, and they work for most homes.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually near the water meter, in the garage, or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off power to that area at the breaker only if you can reach it while standing on dry ground. Otherwise stay out and tell the dispatcher.
  3. Open a faucet at the lowest level of the house to drain pressure from the pipes, and stop running toilets, sinks, showers and the washing machine if sewage is coming up.
  4. Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the wet area, and keep children and pets away from standing water or sewage.
  5. If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911, then call Pacific Gas and Electric. Do that before you call a plumber.
  6. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see. A burst pipe and a backed-up main line need different tools, so the details help us send the right plumber.

Take photos of the damage for your insurer before you start cleanup, but do not wait on photos to shut the water off.

What happens when you call the 24/7 line

The process is the same at 3 a.m. as at noon. You talk to a person, not a menu.

  1. You call the 24/7 emergency line and tell the dispatcher what is happening, where the water is coming from and whether you have already shut it off.
  2. The dispatcher asks about the problem: which fixture, how many drains are affected, whether there is a smell, and whether anyone is in danger.
  3. A licensed plumber is lined up for your address. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and true emergencies go to the front.
  4. Before any work starts, the plumber explains what was found and gives you a clear price. You decide whether to go ahead.
  5. The repair is done, the work area is cleaned up, and you get plain advice on anything that should be watched or planned for later.

What affects the price of a plumbing repair

No two calls are alike, so we do not quote over the phone without facts. These are the things that move a bill up or down.

Where the problem is

A leak in an open crawlspace is a different job from one inside a finished wall or under a concrete slab.

How the pipe is made

Copper, PEX, galvanized steel and cast iron each repair differently, and older materials can take more work to reach safely.

Access and digging

Hillside lots, tight crawlspaces and buried lines under driveways add labor. Digging near the street may also need a permit.

Parts and equipment

A new valve is minor. A water heater, a hydro jet run or a camera inspection brings in larger equipment and materials.

Permits and inspection

Sewer lateral work beyond routine cleaning needs a Central San permit. Permit rules vary, so ask the Lafayette building department.

Timing

After-hours and holiday calls cost more to staff than a weekday visit. Waiting can raise the damage, so weigh both.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not slow leaks or the failed pipe itself, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Whatever the job, call and ask for a clear price before work starts.

Who owns what: EBMUD water and Central San sewers

Know your lines

EBMUD supplies Lafayette water and handles leaks on the street side of the meter. Anything on your side of the meter is yours. If the problem is on EBMUD's side, call EBMUD Emergency Dispatch at 1-866-403-2683 and press 1. To check, shut off all water and see whether the EBMUD leak indicator on the meter keeps moving.

For sewers, Central San owns the main in the street, but you own the entire lateral from the house to that main, including the part under the sidewalk. Root intrusion is the leading cause of backups, and any lateral work other than routine cleaning needs a Central San permit. Our sewer line repair crews know that process.

Hot water and drains that fail on a bad night

A water heater that quits is the most common reason people call after dark. A leaking tank, a tripped temperature and pressure relief valve or a dead element all call for different repairs, and water heater repair is often possible on the spot. If the tank has failed, the plumber can talk through replacement the same visit.

Backed-up drains are the other big one. A plumber can run a cable, then follow with a hydro jet if the line is packed with grease or roots. When the same drain keeps clogging, a camera sewer inspection shows what is actually inside the pipe so you only pay to fix what is broken.

Hidden water is the third. A quiet hiss, a warm patch on the floor or a high bill with no visible leak points to leak detection, using acoustic listening tools to find the pipe before anyone opens a wall. In homes with concrete floors, that can lead to slab leak repair.
