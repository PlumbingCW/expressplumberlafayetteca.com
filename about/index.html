What Lafayette Express Plumbing is

We are a dispatch line, not a showroom. Our address is 3640 Mt. Diablo Blvd, Lafayette, CA 94549, and the phone is staffed around the clock for emergency plumbing calls. When a pipe bursts at 2 a.m. or a sewer line backs up on a holiday, the line is open.

The plumbers we send are licensed plumbers, and the work is done by licensed and insured professionals. We say that plainly because it is the part that matters when someone is working inside your walls and under your floors. You can verify the licence yourself, and the section below shows how.

We handle urgent calls first, and we offer same-day appointments when available for jobs that can wait a few hours. Planned work such as a water heater swap or a repipe also starts with a call, so the plumber can ask the right questions before anyone shows up.

How a call works, start to finish

The process is short on purpose. Here is what happens when you phone the line.

  1. You call and tell us what is happening: where the water is, whether it is still running, and whether anyone is in danger.
  2. We tell you what to do while you wait. That usually means shutting off the right valve, turning off power near water, and keeping people away from wet electrical areas.
  3. We line up a licensed plumber for your address in Lafayette, Orinda, Moraga, Walnut Creek or Pleasant Hill and tell you what to expect next.
  4. The plumber looks at the problem on site, explains the options, and gives you a clear price before work starts. You decide whether to go ahead.

Timing depends on traffic on Highway 24 and Mt. Diablo Boulevard, so we explain on the call what is realistic for your address.

Check any plumber's licence before you let them in

Do this first

California requires plumbing contractors to hold a licence, and you can look one up in a few minutes. Plumbing contractors carry the C-36 classification. Whether you use us or anyone else, check before work begins.

  • Ask the plumber or company for their CSLB licence details.
  • Search it on the CSLB license check from the Contractors State License Board.
  • Confirm the status is active and the classification covers plumbing.
  • Be cautious of anyone who cannot give a number or asks for a large cash payment up front.

What we do not do

Everything starts with a phone call, because an emergency should be one conversation with a person. The website has no forms, booking tools or chat. If you cannot get through, call back.

We do not quote price numbers without understanding the job. A leaking supply line and a failing main line can sound alike on the phone but cost very different amounts. The plumber confirms a clear price on site before work starts.

We are not a gas emergency service. If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911, then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not use matches, lighters or flashlights inside, and call us only after the gas utility says the area is safe. See the PG&E gas leak safety page for their steps. Licensed plumbers on our calls work to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and we do not skip steps it requires.

Local resources every Lafayette homeowner should keep handy

These are the agencies behind your water, your sewer and your permits. Knowing which one owns which problem saves a lot of phone calls.

  • EBMUD supplies water in Lafayette. Read the EBMUD water quality report, which lists Lafayette plant hardness at 16 mg/L, very soft water.
  • For a suspected leak or a high bill, use the EBMUD guide for leaks and high bills. EBMUD handles leaks on the street side of the meter, and you handle the house side.
  • If water is leaking on EBMUD's side of the meter, call EBMUD Emergency Dispatch at 1-866-403-2683 and press 1.
  • Central San serves Lafayette sewers, and the property owner owns the whole lateral to the main. Keep the Central San home guide where you can find it, and learn where your cleanout is.
  • Most sewer lateral work beyond routine cleaning needs a Central San permit. Their permit counter is (925) 229-7371.
  • For permits on other work, ask the City of Lafayette. Its permit page lists water heater repair and replacement at single-family homes as exempt from a building permit, and the city contracts with the Contra Costa County Building Inspection Department. Permit rules vary, so ask the Lafayette building department before you start.
  • Before anyone digs near buried lines, call 811 (USA North 811) first.

A plumber can tell you which of these applies to your problem. If you are not sure whose pipe is leaking, call us and we will talk it through.
