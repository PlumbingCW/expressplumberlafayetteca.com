Water Heater Installation in Lafayette, CA

Plan a new, relocated or upgraded water heater with licensed plumbers who check venting, gas, power and drainage before they quote.

Call (855) 575-2890See all services

Installing a water heater in Lafayette, CA is mostly a planning job. The tank is the easy part. The venting, gas line, electrical circuit, drain and clearances decide whether the new unit will run safely and last.

People call us for different reasons. Some want to move the heater to a garage or attic, convert from gas to a heat pump unit, add a second unit for a remodel, or upgrade to on-demand heat. We look at the site first, explain what the code expects, and give a clear price before work starts.

If your current tank has already failed, replacement covers that faster path. We also take after-hours calls on our 24/7 line, but this page is for installs you can schedule. The T&P valve, discharge pipe and expansion control are set up to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the manufacturer's installation guidelines.

Site checks before anyone orders a unit

We walk the location with a short list. Skipping these is how installs end up needing rework.

  • Venting: can a gas unit vent through the existing flue, or does it need new pipe and a roof or wall termination?
  • Gas line size: a tankless gas unit often needs more capacity than a tank did, so the line may have to be upsized.
  • Electrical capacity: a heat pump or electric unit needs a breaker, wire gauge and panel space that match its nameplate.
  • Clearances: the manufacturer lists distances to walls and to combustibles, and access space for servicing.
  • Drain and pan: where does relief valve discharge go, and where would a leak end up?
  • Expansion tank: needed when the house has a closed system, such as one with a backflow device or pressure reducer.

The install visit, step by step

A clean install follows a sequence. Here is what to expect on the day.

  1. We confirm the plan with you and shut off water, gas and power at the right points.
  2. The old unit, if there is one, is drained and removed. A new stand or pan goes in.
  3. The new heater is set level, then vented, plumbed with new connectors and wired or piped for gas.
  4. We fit the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge line, then add the expansion tank where it is needed.
  5. The tank is braced to the framing as required by California Health and Safety Code section 19211.
  6. We purge air, test for gas leaks and water leaks, set the temperature, and walk you through the controls.

Code and permit questions

Plumbers in California work under the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. It covers relief valve discharge, expansion control, venting and more, and your installer should follow it whether or not an inspection is required.

For single-family homes, the City of Lafayette's permit page lists water heater installation as exempt from a building permit. A relocation, a new gas line, or a multi-family building may be treated differently, so ask the Lafayette building department. Elsewhere in our area, Moraga homeowners should ask their own city building department.

You can also confirm that any contractor holds the right license through the CSLB license check. Plumbing work falls under the C-36 classification.

Gas to heat pump: converting a unit

Planning note

Swapping a gas tank for a hybrid heat pump means capping the old gas line, closing off the flue, and bringing in a circuit. Heat pump units also cool the air around them and drip condensate, so the room needs space and a drain.

If you are weighing an on-demand unit instead, read our page on the tankless water heater to see how the gas and flow requirements differ.

Why Lafayette water helps your choice

EBMUD reports hardness of about 16 mg/L at the plant serving Lafayette, which is very soft. For an installer, that means scale is a minor concern for tank or tankless elements here, and a softener is rarely needed to protect the new heater. The EBMUD water quality report shows the current numbers, and you can test your own tap.

Hilly lots can mean long runs from the street to the house, so tell us if the heater is far from where the gas or water enters, since extra pipe adds both labor and material to the job.

What sets the price of an installation

Price comes from the unit itself, how much pipe, vent and wiring the site needs, whether the panel or gas line must be upgraded, and how hard the location is to reach. Moving a heater to a new room costs more than replacing it in place.

Many homeowner policies do not pay for a planned upgrade, though they may cover sudden water damage, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Call, describe the project, and we will give a clear price before work starts.
