Emergency Drain Cleaning in Lafayette, CA

Licensed plumbers clear stopped kitchen, bath and main drains day or night, with a clear price before work starts.

A drain that will not move water is a problem you feel in every room. Our 24/7 emergency line is staffed for exactly this, and while you wait you can stop running water into the clogged fixture, lay towels around the base, and keep the washer and dishwasher off.

Drain cleaning in Lafayette, CA means clearing the blockage with the right tool for the pipe so the blockage comes out instead of moving down the line. We use hand augers, drum machines and, when the line is big or the clog keeps returning, a camera and a hydro jet.

Below you will find what each kind of clog usually means, what to try before calling, and how we decide between a quick snaking and something heavier. Where a cleanout, trap or vent has to be reset afterward, the work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Standing water in the sink or tub: what to do right now

Do this while you wait

Stop adding water. Every flush, rinse and load of laundry sends more down a pipe that is already full.

  • Turn off the washing machine and dishwasher if either drains to the same line.
  • Do not pour chemical drain opener into standing water. It sits there, burns skin, and makes the job harder for whoever reaches in.
  • Keep children and pets away from water that has backed up from a floor drain or toilet.
  • If water is near outlets or a power strip, leave that area alone and shut off that circuit at the panel only if you can reach it dry.

Kitchen sink clogs

The kitchen is where most stubborn clogs start. Grease cools inside the trap and the horizontal run behind the wall, then catches coffee grounds, rice and food scraps until water crawls or stops.

A plunger helps if the blockage is close, but a double basin with a disposal needs the disposal cleared first. If the unit hums or the clog sits behind it, see our notes on garbage disposal problems before running it again.

We pull the trap, check it for packed grease, then run a cable through the wall line to the branch drain. If the cable comes back coated in sludge, we flush the line so the clog does not rebuild in a few days.

Shower and tub drains that pool at your ankles

Hair and soap scum form a felt-like plug just below the strainer, usually within arm's reach of a small drain snake. That is the easy case, and a hair-catching strainer afterward prevents a repeat.

If the water drains for a minute and then backs up again, the plug is deeper, often at the tub trap or where the branch joins the vent stack. A short cable does not reach there. A powered machine with the correct head cuts through, and we clean the overflow plate too, because it hides its own buildup.

Gurgling when the tub drains is a clue the line is partly blocked beyond this fixture, so we test other drains before we pack up.

Slow bathroom sink drains

A bathroom sink that empties slowly is cheap to fix when you catch it early. Try this order before calling:

  1. Remove the stopper. Many lift out or unscrew from beneath, and hair wraps around the pivot rod.
  2. Wipe out the gunk with a bent wire or a plastic drain tool, not a chemical.
  3. Run hot water for a full minute and watch whether the speed holds.
  4. If it slows again within a day or two, call. The restriction is further down and needs a cable.

A small leak in the drain line

Not every wet cabinet is a clog. A cracked trap, a loose slip nut, or a weeping joint on the drain side leaks only when the fixture is draining, so the cabinet floor stays dry between uses. We run water, watch each joint, and replace the failed fitting while the cabinet is open. Repeated wet stains behind a wall point to a line leak that needs more than a nut tightened, and we say so before opening drywall.

Main line backing up: laundry, floor drains and several fixtures at once

Why this one is different

When the washing machine drain overflows, a shower gurgles after you flush, and the lowest drain in the house bubbles, the problem is almost never a single fixture. The shared line out to the street is blocked.

We find the cleanout, open it, and run a machine from there. Under Central San rules the homeowner owns the whole lateral from the house to the district main, so a blockage out there is yours to clear. Our sewer line cleaning page covers that work in detail.

Local note: what Lafayette conditions change

Central San serves Lafayette, and its guidance names root intrusion as the leading cause of sewer backups. If the same drain has stopped twice in a season, roots in the lateral are a real suspect, and a camera look is worth more than a third round of snaking.

Reading the Central San home guide before a problem starts also helps you find your cleanout. Neighbors in Walnut Creek and Orinda share the same sewer district, so the same checklist applies there.

What drives the cost of clearing a drain

Price depends on which line is blocked, how far the cable has to travel, whether a cleanout is accessible, and whether a camera or jetting is needed after the first pass. A bathroom sink is simpler than a main line with no cleanout. Night and weekend calls are also priced differently from daytime appointments.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not the clog itself or slow seepage, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Call for a clear price before work starts and we will explain what is included.
