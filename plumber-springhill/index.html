Springhill is a wooded hillside neighborhood just north of downtown Lafayette. Streets wind through the trees, many end in dead ends that lead to trailheads, and Briones Regional Park is reached through the neighborhood. Springhill Elementary School is within walking distance, and a monitoring site for Reliez Creek is listed on Springhill Road.

We take plumbing calls from Springhill and the rest of Lafayette. We cannot quote a timing in advance, so ask what to expect when you call. Lamorinda neighbors can use our plumber in Orinda page.

Hillside streets and what to tell us on the call

Local profiles describe single-family houses on wooded lots in the hills, with a mix of ranch, mid-century and newer luxury homes. They also describe winding streets and dead ends. For a plumber, the street matters nearly as much as the pipe. A steep or narrow driveway, a locked gate or a dead end with little room to turn around changes which equipment can reach your house.

So when you call, say what the access is like. Mention the slope of the drive, where a vehicle can park and whether anyone will be home. That small detail lets the dispatcher plan the visit properly.

What a Springhill call usually starts with

Most calls from a hillside house fall into a few groups, and each has a simple first step.

Slow or backed-up drains

Stop water use and check the cleanout. See drain cleaning for what a clearing visit covers.

Gas, earthquakes and water heaters on a hill

Safety first

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced or anchored against earthquake movement. If you smell gas at your water heater, leave the building, call 911, then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not use matches, flashlights or switches near the smell. Call PG&E to restore gas after it is shut off.

What the age data does and does not tell you

One neighborhood data profile lists a median year built of 1959 for the Springhill area. Treat that as a rough guide to when many houses were built, not a statement about the pipes inside yours. Repairs and remodels over the decades mean the only dependable answer comes from looking at the pipes and putting a camera in the drain.

If you are weighing whether to replace aging supply lines, ask a plumber to show you what is actually there first. Repiping is a large job and should follow evidence, not a guess.

Check the plumber before the work starts

Worth knowing

In California, plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is Part 5 of Title 24 of the California Code of Regulations and is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Plumbing contractors hold a C-36 classification, and you can look up any contractor's license on the Contractors State License Board site. Do that before agreeing to a water heater swap or a repipe.
