Tankless Water Heater in Lafayette, CA

Install, repair and service for on-demand water heaters, from gas line sizing to descaling and error codes.

Call (855) 575-2890See all services

A tankless water heater gives you hot water only when a tap is open, with no storage tank to rust or flood. We install, repair and service these units in Lafayette, CA, and the work splits into three parts: sizing the unit, keeping it clean, and fixing it when an error code appears.

Most trouble traces back to gas supply, flow, or maintenance that was skipped. This page walks through each, so you know what a good setup looks like and when a call makes sense. Our 24/7 line is open if a unit fails and leaves you without hot water. Gas, venting and condensate drain details follow the manufacturer's instructions and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

How an on-demand heater makes hot water

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor tells the unit to fire. A gas model lights a burner under a heat exchanger, and an electric model energizes heating elements, and either way the water is heated as it passes through.

That is why flow rate matters. Each unit has a maximum number of gallons per minute it can raise by a given temperature, and running a shower, dishwasher and laundry at once can exceed it. The result is water that goes lukewarm, not cold.

Gas or electric, and what each needs

Gas tankless

Delivers the highest flow. Needs a properly sized gas line, a dedicated vent, and a condensate drain on condensing models.

Electric tankless

Compact and ventless. Draws a large load, so the panel and wire must be able to carry it.

Gas line sizing

A tankless burner uses much more gas at once than a tank. An undersized line starves it and causes shutdowns.

Flow sizing

Add up the fixtures you run together, then match the unit's rated flow at your coldest incoming water.

Flushing and descaling: the part people skip

Mineral scale coats the heat exchanger and narrows its passages. The unit then runs hotter, wastes energy, and eventually throws an error or fails. A flush pushes a descaling solution, usually a mild acid such as vinegar or a manufacturer-approved product, through the unit with a small pump and two hoses.

How often depends on your water and on the manufacturer. Many makers call for a yearly flush, and some warranties require proof of it. Read your unit's manual and follow its schedule.

EBMUD Lafayette water is very soft, at about 16 mg/L hardness, so scale usually builds slowly here. That is a reason the interval may feel long, not a reason to skip it, because the manufacturer's schedule and the inlet screen still need attention. The EBMUD water quality report has the current figures.

Keep it running on cold nights

Freeze note

Winters here are mild, so frozen tankless units are rare. Still, exposed vent terminations and water lines on an outside wall can get cold on a clear night, and some units have built-in freeze protection that only works while powered. Insulate exposed pipes and leave the unit plugged in.

Repairing a tankless unit

Repair visits follow a consistent order.

  1. We read the error code, check the history, and ask what changed before it failed.
  2. Gas pressure, venting, and the inlet filter are checked, since they cause many faults.
  3. The unit is flushed if scale is suspected, and sensors, igniters or the flow valve are tested.
  4. If a part has failed, we replace it, test fire the unit, and confirm temperature at several taps.
  5. If the heat exchanger is cracked or the unit is near the end of life, we explain the cost of a new one against water heater repair for a tank.

What shapes the price of tankless work

Installs depend on the model, gas line upsizing, venting, electrical upgrades and how far the unit sits from existing pipes. Service pricing depends on the part that failed and whether the unit has ever been flushed. A first flush on a neglected unit takes longer.
