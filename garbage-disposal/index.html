Emergency Garbage Disposal in Lafayette, CA

A dead, humming or leaking disposal can take the whole kitchen out of use. Our licensed plumbers answer 24/7.

Call (855) 575-2890See all services

If your disposal hums but will not spin, or water is dripping from the unit, switch it off at the wall and do not put your hand in the drain. Our emergency line runs 24/7 for garbage disposal problems in Lafayette, and you can call while the power is off.

A disposal can fail on its own, or it can be the visible symptom of a clog further down the kitchen drain. Telling those apart saves you from replacing a unit that was never the problem.

Read on for fast checks, leak diagnosis, replacement choices and what to keep out of the hopper.

Humming, jammed or silent: what to check before you call

Cut power first. Turn the switch off and, if you can, unplug the unit or flip its breaker.

  • Never reach into the hopper. Use tongs or pliers to pull out a spoon, bone or bottle cap you can see.
  • Insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket in the center of the bottom and work it back and forth to free the flywheel.
  • Press the red reset button on the underside once the unit has cooled.
  • Restore power and run cold water. If the motor still hums with no spin, the unit is likely seized.
  • If it is silent and the reset does not click, the problem may be the outlet, the switch or the motor.

If any of these steps feel unsafe, stop and call.

Where a leaking disposal drips from

The location of the drip tells you the repair.

Leak locationLikely causeUsual fix
Top, under the sink flange.Worn gasket or loose mounting ring.Reseat the flange with fresh plumber's putty and gasket.
Side, at the dishwasher hose.Loose clamp or cracked hose.Replace clamp or hose.
Side, at the drain outlet.Loose bolts or bad gasket.Tighten or replace the gasket.
Bottom of the unit.Cracked housing or failed seal.Replace the disposal.

A leak from the bottom of the body almost never repairs. Seal failures inside the housing mean a new unit.

Backing up into the other sink basin

When you run the disposal and water rises in the second basin, the blockage is past the disposal. The trap or the kitchen branch line is clogged, usually with grease and food paste that coated the pipe over time. The disposal pushes more waste into the same narrowing line.

Dishwashers drain through the disposal or the same trap arm. If the dishwasher leaves standing water, or sink water backs into the machine, expect the clog to be in that shared drain. This is a job for drain cleaning, with an auger or hydro jet, and it often fixes the disposal complaint too.

Clearing the kitchen branch is quicker than most people expect, as long as the main line is clear.

Replacing a disposal: what to choose

Size, mounting and wiring all matter when we swap a unit.

Horsepower

A one-half horsepower unit suits light use. Three-quarter or one horsepower grinds tougher scraps and runs quieter.

Mounting and flange

Many sinks fit a standard three-bolt mount. We check that the flange and gasket match your sink and the drain arm height.

Electrical

Most units use a switched outlet under the sink or a hardwired box. We do not rewire beyond plumbing scope, and can point you to an electrician if the circuit needs work.

Dishwasher knockout

New disposals have a plug in the dishwasher port that must be punched out, or the dishwasher will not drain. We do that and reattach the hose.

A plumber's quick-fix list when the unit dies

If the unit has failed and you are waiting on us, these steps keep the kitchen usable.

  1. Turn off the disposal switch and the breaker if you suspect an electrical fault.
  2. Close both angle stops under the sink if water is dripping, or the main if they stick.
  3. Remove standing water with a cup or small bucket, and put a pan under the unit.
  4. Wash dishes in a tub and empty it outside until the drain is clear.
  5. Do not pour drain chemicals into a jammed disposal. They sit in the unit and splash onto whoever services it.

What belongs in the hopper, and what does not

Disposals handle soft food scraps and small amounts of cooked leftovers. Grease, fat and cooking oil are the worst offenders, because they cool and coat the drain wall. Coffee grounds pack into sludge. Potato peels, rice and pasta swell and turn into paste. Bones, fruit pits, corn husks and fibrous stalks wear out the blades or jam them.

Run cold water for 15 seconds before and after use, and feed scraps in slowly rather than all at once. If the unit smells, ice cubes and a citrus peel can help, though they do nothing for a drain that is already narrowed.
