Emergency Toilet Repair in Lafayette, CA

An overflowing or leaking toilet needs a fast fix. Licensed plumbers answer our 24/7 line and repair or replace what failed.

Call (855) 575-2890See all services

If your toilet is overflowing right now, reach behind it and turn the oval handle on the supply valve clockwise. Our 24/7 emergency line is open for toilet repair in Lafayette, so call while the water is stopped and keep towels at the base.

Most toilet problems come from a handful of parts: the flapper, the fill valve, the wax ring under the bowl, or a blockage downstream. Each has a different fix, and guessing wrong wastes a weekend.

Below you will find how to stop an overflow, how to tell a toilet problem from a main line problem, and what repair or replacement involves. Replacement parts and any flange work follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so the seal and the fixture height are set to standard.

Toilet overflowing: stop the water, then protect the floor

Work through these in order. They take under a minute if the valve is not stuck.

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop water entering the bowl, or lift the float to stop the fill.
  2. Close the supply valve at the wall by turning it clockwise. If it will not budge, shut off the main water instead.
  3. Do not flush again. Scoop excess bowl water into a bucket, and lay towels around the base.
  4. Keep power strips, floor lamps and cords away from wet floors, and keep children out of the room.
  5. If sewage is coming up, avoid contact, wear gloves, and call. Wet floors near bathroom outlets are a shock risk.

Running, hissing or phantom flushing

A toilet that runs on or refills every few minutes is almost always a worn flapper or a fill valve that no longer shuts off cleanly. You can test the flapper by adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank and waiting 15 minutes. If color shows up in the bowl, water is leaking past the seal.

The fill valve is the tall assembly on the left side of most tanks. When it fails, you may hear a hiss, see water running over the overflow tube, or notice a long refill cycle. Both parts are inexpensive, and replacing them is a routine repair.

A tank that sweats in summer is not a leak. Water that rises from the base is.

Leaking at the base or rocking on the floor

A toilet seals to the drain flange with a wax ring. If water seeps out around the base when you flush, or the bowl moves under you, that ring has usually failed or the toilet has loosened. The leak is often slow, which is why people find soft flooring before they find the cause.

We pull the toilet, clean the old wax, check the flange for cracks or a low height, and set a new ring with fresh bolts. If the flange is broken or rusted, we repair it so the bowl sits level and tight.

Rot under the bowl is possible after a long leak. We will tell you what we see before we reset the toilet.

When the clog keeps coming back

Check this

A plunger solves a one-time blockage. If the toilet clogs again within days, the problem is likely deeper than the trap.

Run the nearest sink or tub. If they gurgle, drain slowly, or if the shower backs up when you flush, the blockage is in a shared line or the main. That calls for drain cleaning with an auger or a camera, not another toilet repair. Never mix drain chemicals with a plunger, because splashback burns skin.

Repair or replace: how we decide

Most leaks and running toilets are worth repairing. A new flapper, fill valve or wax ring costs far less than a new toilet, and the bowl stays in service.

Replacement makes more sense for a cracked bowl or tank, repeated clogs from an old high-water-use design, or a bowl that has been pulled and reset many times. We can set a new unit as part of broader plumbing installation work.

Water-saving toilets use less per flush, which is a useful long-term choice, though a modern low-flow bowl is no cure for a blocked main line.

Toilet repair cost: what moves the price

Price depends on which part failed, whether the supply valve needs replacement, whether the flange or subfloor is damaged, and whether you want a new toilet. An after-hours call and a hard-to-reach tank also affect the total.

If a leak has soaked flooring or drywall, ask your insurer what is covered. Many homeowner policies cover sudden overflow damage but not slow seepage or the worn part itself, so read your policy.

If the bathroom sink is also dripping or its shutoff is corroded, we can handle that in the same visit through faucet repair. Call and describe the symptom for a clear price before work starts.
