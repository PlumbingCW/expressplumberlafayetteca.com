How Burton Valley was built, and why it matters to your pipes

Local reporting describes the valley as pear and walnut orchards until the 1920s. The Sacramento Northern Railroad ran through the area, along the line where the Lafayette-Moraga trail runs now. After the railroad closed in the 1950s the land became suburban. A 1957 county photograph shows houses going up along St. Mary's Road near Glenside Drive, and a second phase of building followed in the early 1970s.

Two building phases mean two houses on the same street can differ inside, even when they look alike from the curb. A neighbor's repair history is not a record of what is in your walls. The reliable ways to know are to look at the visible pipes at the water heater and under the sinks, and to have a camera look into the drain when something keeps backing up.

The same reporting says the homes are mostly single-story ranch houses. On a one-level house the water heater, the main shutoff and the sewer cleanout are often close to the ground, so you can find them on a calm day. That is worth doing now, not during a leak.

Three things to find before you need a plumber

Ten minutes on a dry afternoon can save a lot of confusion later.

  • The main water shutoff for the house, so you can stop a leak at once.
  • The shutoff and the relief valve on your water heater, and where the discharge pipe ends.
  • The sewer cleanout cap, which Central San says every owner should know how to find.

Tell everyone in the house where these are. A shutoff nobody can find does not help at midnight.

Creeks, trees and the sewer lateral you own

Know your lines

Local coverage names Las Trampas Creek and Grizzly Creek among Burton Valley's features. A creek nearby does not mean your sewer has a problem. What matters is the line between your house and the district main. Central San says the property owner owns that whole lateral, including the part under the sidewalk or street.

Central San also says root intrusion is the leading cause of sewer backups, and that lateral work other than routine cleaning needs a Central San permit. If you have mature trees near the line and your drains keep backing up, a camera inspection tells you where the roots are before anyone digs.

If a drain backs up tonight

  1. Stop running water and do not flush, since it has nowhere to go.
  2. Check whether one fixture or several are affected.
  3. Skip chemical drain openers, which can splash on whoever works on the line next.
  4. Call and tell us which fixtures are affected and when it started.
