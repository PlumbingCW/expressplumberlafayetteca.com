Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Lafayette, CA

A failed tank can flood a garage or closet in hours. Licensed plumbers swap it out, with same-day appointments when available.

If your tank has split open or the water coming out of the tap is brown, you need a new heater and you need the flooding stopped first. We answer the 24/7 emergency line for water heater replacement in Lafayette, CA. While you wait, shut the cold supply valve, turn off the power or gas, and keep everyone away from the wet floor.

Replacement is a bigger job than a repair, so the choices matter: fuel type, tank size, where the unit sits, and what gets upgraded while the old one is out. We explain the options in plain terms and give a clear price before work starts.

If the unit is still working and only has a bad part, a repair may cost less than a new tank. This page is for the cases where it does not. The replacement tank is installed to the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the earthquake bracing standards in California Health and Safety Code 19211.

Burst tank and flooding: what to do first

Do this first

A burst tank can release dozens of gallons quickly, and the water goes wherever the floor slopes.

  • Close the cold supply valve on top of the heater, or the main shutoff for the house if that valve is stuck.
  • Electric unit: turn off its breaker. Gas unit: turn the gas control to off.
  • Gas smell? Leave, call 911, then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not stay to mop.
  • Photograph the water and the label on the tank for your insurer.
  • Call us with the tank size, fuel type, and where it is installed.

Signs the old tank is finished

Rusty or cloudy hot water that clears when you run only the cold tap means the inside of the tank is corroding. Puddles under the shell, wet insulation and rust streaks near the bottom seam say the same thing.

A tank that has been repaired more than once in a short stretch is also telling you something. Parts are cheap, but each failure usually means the tank is wearing out as a whole.

Choosing size and fuel before the old tank comes out

Gas tanks recover quickly and keep working if the power drops, while electric tanks need only a circuit and no vent. Hybrid heat pump heaters use far less electricity but need open air space around them and a drain for condensate, so they are often a better fit for a garage than a closet.

Size by household, not by habit. A tank that is too small gives you cold showers on laundry day, and one that is too big costs more to buy and keep hot. Tell us how many people live in the house and we can match the size.

If you are thinking of going tankless, the tankless water heater page explains the extra gas and venting work.

What a replacement visit includes

The tank itself is only part of the job. A proper swap usually covers the following.

  • Draining the old unit and disconnecting it safely, including the gas line or electric whip.
  • New flexible water connectors and, on gas units, a new gas connector and a leak test at each joint.
  • A new temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot.
  • An expansion tank when the system is closed and one is needed.
  • A drain pan and pan drain where the tank sits above living space.
  • Seismic bracing straps under California Health and Safety Code section 19211.
  • Hauling away the old tank and a hot-water test at the fixtures.

Permits and the City of Lafayette

Local note

The City of Lafayette lists plumbing and mechanical permits for water heater repair, replacement or installation at single-family homes as exempt from a building permit. That is the city's own permit page, and it applies to single-family houses. Condos, townhomes, and anything beyond a straight swap can differ, so ask the Lafayette building department before you plan on skipping paperwork.

The county handles inspections for the city, so questions about a particular address go to the building department, not to us.

Getting more years out of the next tank

The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the tank does not. Replacing it every few years, and flushing sediment from the base on a regular schedule, is the cheapest way to extend a tank's life.

EBMUD's Lafayette water is very soft at 16 mg/L hardness, so scale is less of a worry here, though soft water can use up an anode rod faster than hard water does. Check the rod instead of assuming it is fine. The EBMUD water quality report has the full numbers.

If you are in nearby Orinda, the same advice holds for EBMUD water there.

How replacement pricing is worked out

Three things move the total most: the unit you choose, the condition of the space around it, and any code upgrades the old install is missing. A tight closet, a long gas run, an out-of-date vent, or the need to add bracing or a pan all add labor.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not the failed heater, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Call, and we will give a clear price before work starts.
