Water Softener and Filtration in Lafayette, CA

Straight advice first: most Lafayette homes do not need a softener. Filters for taste or chloramine are a different question.

Call (855) 575-2890See all services

Most homes in Lafayette, CA do not need a water softener for hardness. EBMUD reports that water from its Lafayette treatment plant is very soft, about 16 mg/L or roughly 0.9 grains per gallon, and a salt softener is built to fix hard water you do not have.

That does not mean filtration is pointless. Plenty of people want better taste, less chlorine-type smell, chloramine removal for an aquarium, or clean water at one tap. We are licensed plumbers, and we will tell you plainly whether a system makes sense before you buy one.

We also service existing softeners, brine valves and bypass plumbing for homes that came with one. Bypass valves, drain connections and backflow protection follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so the install is done to a clear standard.

Read your tap before you buy a system

Spend an hour on this before any salesperson visits. It can save you a system you will never use.

  1. Read the EBMUD annual water quality report and find the treatment plant that serves your address.
  2. Note hardness, pH and disinfectant listed for that plant.
  3. Check for real symptoms in your own house: white crust on faucets, scale in a kettle, spotting on glasses.
  4. Buy a simple hardness test strip or kit and test a cold tap, because a number on your hands beats a number on a brochure.
  5. If your water tests soft and you have no scale, a softener will not change much, and you can focus on the taste or filtering goals instead.

What a softener would change here, and what it would not

A salt-based softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium. That matters only when those minerals are high.

EBMUD says to divide mg/L by 17 to get grains per gallon, which is how 16 mg/L becomes about 0.9. At that level, scale buildup is typically low, so a salt softener usually adds salt, maintenance and drain water without a clear benefit.

Orinda and Walnut Creek plants report similar hardness, at 16 to 18 and 15 to 16 mg/L. The numbers above are for the Lafayette plant, and your address may be served differently, so confirm it in the report.

When filtration still makes sense

These goals are real, and none of them require a softener.

Better taste

A carbon filter at the kitchen tap improves flavor for many people who dislike the taste of treated water.

Aquariums and fish

Chloramine is harmful to fish, so aquarium owners use a conditioner or carbon treatment made for chloramine.

Drinking water at one tap

An under-sink reverse osmosis unit gives a separate faucet for drinking and cooking.

Whole-house sediment and carbon

A cartridge filter at the main catches grit, and a carbon stage reduces taste and odor throughout the house.

Chloramine in plain terms

What chloramine is

Chloramine is a disinfectant made from chlorine and ammonia that many water utilities use because it lasts longer in the pipes. EBMUD reports about 2.6 mg/L at the Lafayette plant.

Activated carbon can reduce chloramine when the filter is sized and the flow is slow enough, though performance varies by product, so check the manufacturer's rating. Boiling does not remove it, and it matters for fish tanks and some kidney dialysis, so ask a doctor if that applies to you.

Servicing a softener you already own

Some homes came with a softener, and some owners are unsure they need it.

  • Check the brine tank for salt bridges, mushy salt or a gummed-up bottom.
  • Ask us to check the control valve, resin and bypass plumbing if the unit cycles constantly or leaks.
  • Test the water after the softener. If it is no different from before, you may not need it running.
  • Switch it to bypass or replace with a filter if the report and tests show soft water, and consider salt consumption and drain discharge.
  • Replace worn valves, hoses and fittings only after confirming the unit is worth keeping.

Cost factors for filters and softener service

Price depends on the system type, flow capacity, filter media, whether a drain line or electrical outlet is needed, and how easy the access is. Under-sink units are simpler than whole-house systems, which need cuts into the main line, bypass valves and room for the tanks.

Ongoing costs matter too: cartridges, membranes, salt and replacement schedules. Insurance rarely covers optional treatment systems, so plan for the full cost. Call for a clear price before work starts, and we will list the parts and the ongoing upkeep so nothing surprises you.
