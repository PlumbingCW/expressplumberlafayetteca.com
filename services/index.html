How to choose the right service

Start with the symptom, not the service name. Water spreading on a floor, a ceiling stain that grows, or a meter dial that moves with everything off all point to a leak. A sudden spray or soaked wall is a burst pipe. A slow, hidden loss, such as a warm spot underfoot, is a job for leak detection first, before any digging.

For drains, the number of affected fixtures tells you a lot. One slow sink usually means a local clog and drain cleaning is enough. If a tub gurgles when the washer drains, or several fixtures back up together, the problem is in the main line. That calls for sewer line cleaning, sometimes with hydro jetting when grease or roots coat the pipe walls. A camera view through sewer inspection helps when backups repeat.

Water heaters split into three paths. A tank that leaks, trips a relief valve or loses heat may be fixed through water heater repair. A tank at the end of its life needs water heater replacement. If you are choosing a new type, tankless water heater work includes gas, venting and sizing questions that deserve a planned visit.

Commercial buildings have their own rules for grease lines, restrooms and shared drains, so commercial plumbing is separate from home service. If you manage a shop or restaurant on a busy street, tell the dispatcher it is a business so the right equipment comes along.

Old supply pipes that keep springing leaks may justify repiping instead of another patch. If you are unsure whether a repair or a replacement makes sense, ask the plumber to explain both before you decide. You should never be pushed into the larger job without seeing why.

Local ownership rules affect the choice as well. EBMUD fixes leaks on the street side of the meter, and you own everything past it.

Central San owns the sewer main, but the property owner owns the whole lateral to the house, so sewer line repair on that lateral is your responsibility and needs a Central San permit. Gas smells are different from every service here: leave, call 911, and call Pacific Gas and Electric before you call us. Whichever service fits, the work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
