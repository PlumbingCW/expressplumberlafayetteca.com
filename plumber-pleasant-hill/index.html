Postwar streets, older plumbing: what the housing era means

Pleasant Hill grew up in the postwar years, and Gregory Gardens was developed in 1950. Houses from that period are decades old now, and the same is true of the drain lines and supply pipes that came with them. Older homes in general may have cast iron, clay or galvanized pipes that wear out slowly and then fail suddenly.

That does not mean every house has trouble. It means that when a drain backs up or a toilet runs constantly, the cause is more likely to be wear than a one-off accident. Ask for a camera check of the line while we are already in the yard, so you learn the pipe's condition before the next backup.

Toilet trouble tonight: step by step

Most toilet calls are one of three things: a clog, a failing flapper or fill valve, or a blocked drain line behind it.

  1. If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the supply valve behind the toilet.
  2. Do not flush again. Water that rose once will rise again.
  3. If other drains gurgle or the tub fills when you flush, the main line is likely blocked.
  4. Mop standing water, and keep the bathroom door closed against pets.
  5. Call us. A running or leaking toilet goes to toilet repair, while a main-line blockage is a job for sewer line cleaning.

Sewer lateral basics for Pleasant Hill property owners

Worth knowing

Central San serves Pleasant Hill sewer. The owner of the property owns the entire lateral to the district main, including the section under the sidewalk or street. Roots cause more backups than anything else, which is why a hydro jet cleaning is often the first fix.

Beyond routine cleaning, lateral work needs a Central San permit, and work in the street needs a city encroachment permit. The Central San home guide explains the owner's role. For water quality questions, ask your water utility.
