Downtown Lafayette is built around Mt. Diablo Boulevard and Moraga Road, with the Lafayette BART station beside Highway 24. The City of Lafayette's Downtown Specific Plan covers about 323 acres of residential and commercial land. That mix of shops, offices and homes in a compact area shapes the plumbing calls that come from it.

We take plumbing calls from downtown businesses, condominium owners and nearby homes across Lafayette. We cannot quote a timing in advance, so ask what to expect when you call. Businesses just east of town can use our plumber in Walnut Creek page.

A compact downtown with shops, offices and homes

The Downtown Lafayette Specific Plan is a City framework for land use, urban design and circulation, meant to guide public and private development downtown over about 20 years. The plan area is residential and commercial, with Mt. Diablo Boulevard and Moraga Road named as its cross streets and the Happy Valley, Lafayette and Las Trampas creeks nearby.

Shops, restaurants and offices sit close to condominiums. In that setting one blocked drain can affect several units, and the owner may not be the person who is able to fix it. Knowing who owns which pipe is the first step in any downtown plumbing call.

Town Center, BART and what the construction means for access

The City of Lafayette awarded a contract on October 17, 2025 for the Lafayette Town Center Pathway and BART Bike Station Project. It links the BART station to the existing Town Center pathway and adds a public plaza on 19 BART permit parking spaces, a bike station and an art installation. Planning began in 2014 during review of the Town Center condominium project, and the section leading to BART was completed in 2018.

For a downtown plumbing call, the takeaway is practical. Street work and temporary closures near the station can change where a crew can park, so tell the dispatcher your exact address and the nearest cross street.

Call 811 before digging near a downtown building

Before anyone digs

Buried water, gas, sewer and utility lines run close together downtown. Call 811, the USA North 811 line for Northern California, before any digging. Digging in a street or public right of way also needs a City encroachment permit.

Steps for a kitchen or restroom backup

  1. Stop using the affected fixtures and tell neighbors on the same line.
  2. Find the nearest cleanout and clear the area around it.
  3. Note which fixtures are slow or gurgling.
  4. Contact the building owner or association if the line is shared.
  5. Call us with the address and what you noticed.
